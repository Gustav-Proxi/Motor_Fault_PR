\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

A 100\,Hz smartphone sensor keeps some fault information and hides the rest. Lubrication loss
\textbf{survives}: B1 separates from every class through impulsiveness, with no defect frequency
needed, and survives a change of load. The rotor-bar signature is \textbf{hidden}: the sidebands
fall outside the band our feature integrates and, at 50\,Hz supply, coincide with aliased shaft
harmonics. B3 and V lie \textbf{above Nyquist}, and what separates them weakly is waveform
asymmetry, slip and aliased mechanical harmonics, not their own signatures. Random splits overstate
accuracy, LDA's in-sample separability is recording identity, and Kernel PCA, DTW and tuning do not
help. Clean-trained models collapse under modest noise or dropout, and an unseen load needs
per-condition normalisation.

\textbf{Limitations and improvements.} Each class is recorded once per condition, on one motor
and one phone, so recording-to-recording variation is barely sampled; this, not model capacity,
limits accuracy. More recordings per class and condition, more motors and phones, sideband
features at the measured $f_p$, training on corrupted data and a sensor above 200\,Hz would show
which parts of the boundary belong to the faults and which to this setup. Bearing frequencies
assume a 6204-type bearing. Every table and figure regenerates from the repository.
