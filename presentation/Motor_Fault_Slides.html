Which motor faults survive a 100 Hz phone sensor?

Bandwidth-limited pattern recognition on smartphone vibration: which class structure survives, which projection keeps it, and what happens under sensor noise and unseen operating conditions (Track 2).

Vaishak G Kumar  ·  Sanika Vilas Najan
Department of Engineering Science (AI), University at Buffalo · Fall 2026
Motor faults at 100 Hz
Motors use about half of the world's electricity, and vibration monitoring catches their faults early, but it normally needs kilohertz accelerometers. We asked what a phone lying on the motor can still see when it samples at only 100 Hz.
01 · The problem

The textbook fault signatures live above 50 Hz

  • Electric motors use 53% of the world's electricity (72% in industry).
  • Standard diagnostics demodulate bearing frequencies from accelerometers at kHz rates; the common benchmark (Case Western) is sampled at 12 and 48 kHz.
  • A phone is already at every machine, but logs at about 100 Hz. Anything above the 50 Hz Nyquist limit is removed or folded back into the band.

Our question

Not “which classifier is best”, but: which class structure survives the truncation, which projection preserves it, and does it hold up when the sensor degrades or the operating condition changes?

IEA 4E EMSA (2025); Smith & Randall, MSSP (2015).
Motor faults at 100 Hz
The bearing outer-race frequency and the voltage-imbalance signature sit above 50 Hz, so a 100 Hz phone cannot see them directly. The rotor-bar sidebands and the impulsiveness of poor lubrication sit below. So the interesting question is which structure survives, not which classifier wins.

Each cell is one ~15-minute recording (90,000 samples per channel). One recording per class and condition is the dataset's main limitation.

Sensor

iPhone 15 Pro Max lying on a squirrel-cage induction motor; 6 inertial channels at 100 Hz (raw and gravity-compensated acceleration, x/y/z).

Windows

1,024 samples (10.24 s) with a 512-sample step: 6,264 labelled windows, 174 per recording. Mean removed per window; amplitudes kept, since vibration energy is discriminative.

Ertarğın et al., Data in Brief 67 (2026); public, CC BY 4.0.
Motor faults at 100 Hz
Six classes: healthy, two levels of lubrication loss, a cracked outer race, voltage imbalance and a broken rotor bar. Each is recorded once at three supply frequencies, unloaded and loaded. Because overlapping windows from one recording are near-copies, the unit of generalisation is the recording, not the window.
03 · Method

126 hand-written features, five front-ends, six classifiers

1 · features (per channel × 6)

21 features → 126

8 time-domain: RMS, variance, skewness, kurtosis, crest, peak-to-peak, shape and impulse factor.

13 spectral, relative to the shaft speed f₁: energy at f₁ and 2f₁, sideband band, four band powers, Welch centroid / spread / entropy, spectral kurtosis, slip.

2 · front-end (written by hand)

Reduce 126 → k

  • none (raw 126-D)
  • PCA, 95% variance · PCA-5
  • Fisher LDA-5, Ledoit–Wolf shrinkage
  • RBF Kernel PCA-5 (γ = 1/126)
3 · classifier (scikit-learn)

30 pipelines

  • k-NN (Euclidean, Mahalanobis)
  • Gaussian naive Bayes
  • linear and RBF SVM
  • random forest (300 trees)

Protocol: hold out whole recordings

Grouped 6-fold CV: every test fold holds one unseen recording per class; everything (scaling, projection, classifier) is fitted on the training fold only. Five seeds, macro-F1 mean ± std, chance 0.17. Plus: sensor-corruption sweeps, unseen speed / load splits, Subspace Alignment, nested tuning and DTW.

Motor faults at 100 Hz
Features are deliberately classical and interpretable. PCA, LDA and Kernel PCA are implemented by hand. The key design choice is the grouped split: the six recordings of each class are dealt one per fold, so the model is always tested on a recording it has never seen.

Why

Overlapping windows of one recording are near-copies. A random split puts copies of the same recording on both sides, so the model recognises the recording, not the fault.

So

Every number from here on holds out whole recordings and is averaged over five seeds; fold-to-fold spread reaches ±0.2.

Same effect reported for bearing data: Wheat et al., IEEE Access (2024).

This is the first result and the reason for our protocol. With a random split almost every model is perfect. Holding out conditions or recordings drops the best model to 0.56 and 0.47, about three times chance.

06 · Result 3: what survives at 100 Hz

Lubrication loss survives. The rotor bar hides in the fold.

B1 survives

Separates from every class (held-out J 2.2–7.4) through impulsiveness: kurtosis (J 7.5 vs H), peak-to-peak, impulse factor. No defect frequency needed. Recall 0.95.

R is hidden

At 50 Hz supply its sidebands land exactly on aliased shaft harmonics: f₁ + fp = 100 − 3f₁, the alias of 3f₁. Its only cue is slip under load, hence R ↔ B2 confusion (0.34).

B3 V above Nyquist

Separated only weakly and indirectly: skewness for B3 (J 1.1), energy at aliased shaft harmonics (J 2.75) and slip for V.

Motor faults at 100 Hz · separability numbers: seed 0
The projections show the held-out recordings landing beside LDA's tight training clusters. Per class, B1 is recognised almost perfectly because poor lubrication makes the vibration impulsive. The rotor-bar sidebands coincide with aliased shaft harmonics at 50 Hz supply, so a 100 Hz sensor cannot tell them apart.
07 · Result 4: sensor corruption

Trained on clean data, models collapse under 10% noise

Corrupted alike (top)

Noise and dropout barely matter for raw and LDA, even at 50%. Gain drift hurts LDA most: 0.45 → 0.35.

Clean train, corrupted test (bottom)

10% noise or dropout drops the projected front-ends to 0.17–0.26 and Kernel PCA to 0.06–0.17. Raw features degrade least (0.32 at every dropout level).

Why

Corruption shifts kurtosis and entropy rather than erasing the classes. Projections fitted on clean data are the most brittle, so train on corrupted data.

Motor faults at 100 Hz · 5 seeds, ±1 std
We corrupt whole raw recordings before feature extraction: white noise, Bluetooth-style dropout with interpolation, and per-axis gain drift. If training and test are corrupted alike, accuracy barely moves. If the model is trained clean, modest noise or dropout is enough to break it.

Testing at 40 Hz after training on 30 and 50 Hz is interpolation, and it beats grouped CV at 0.66. A new load is the hard shift: both PCA front-ends collapse to about 0.14. Normalising each condition separately recovers most of it; Subspace Alignment adds little.
09 · Result 6: accuracy vs hyperparameters

Only the projected dimension matters

Projected dimension

PCA rises from 0.14 at k = 1 to 0.42 at k ≥ 12; Kernel PCA reaches 0.37 at k = 10, so five dimensions are too few for both. LDA peaks at k = 3 (0.46).

Classifier settings

The linear SVM is flat for C ≥ 0.01 and the neighbour count barely moves k-NN. Nested tuning: −0.03 to +0.04.

Kernel PCA and DTW

Kernel PCA keeps PCA-level held-out J (10.4 vs 10.1) yet classifies at 0.28. DTW scores 0.28 vs 0.26 without warping: a steady run has nothing to align.

Motor faults at 100 Hz · sweep: 5 seeds · tuning and DTW: seed 0
Each panel changes one hyperparameter and keeps the rest at the defaults, scored on held-out recordings over five seeds. Only the number of projected dimensions matters: PCA and Kernel PCA need more than five. The classifier settings and nested tuning barely move the result, and DTW adds nothing on steady runs. The limit is recording-to-recording variation, not model capacity.
10 · Conclusions

The result is a boundary, not an accuracy

Survives: lubrication loss, through impulsiveness, even across a load change.

Hidden: rotor-bar sidebands coincide with aliased shaft harmonics at 50 Hz supply.

Above Nyquist: cracked race and voltage imbalance; only weak, indirect cues remain.

Method: random splits overstate accuracy (1.00 vs 0.47); in-sample LDA separability is recording identity; clean-trained models break under 10% noise; an unseen load needs per-condition normalisation.

Limitations and next steps

  • One recording per class and condition, one motor, one phone: recording-to-recording variation is barely sampled.
  • More recordings, sideband features at the measured fp, training on corrupted data, a sensor above 200 Hz.
